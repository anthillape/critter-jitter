Where the nutrients are

Log scale: each gridline is ten times the one below. Dashed lines are the environment. Grass includes seeds and algae its drifting spores; animals include their stomachs, unborn young and rotting bodies. Hover to read values.

Where the energy is

Log scale. Energy isn't stored by the ground or water (sunlight is used or lost each tick), so it's all in living things: plants' stored energy (with seeds and spores) and animals' fat, undigested food and what they've passed to unborn young. Hover to read values.

        Info cards

        The cards under the map scroll on their own. Click a card's title to collapse or expand it; drag the title to move the card, and it snaps into place when you let go. The layout is remembered.

        Map

        Brown ground is lighter on higher ground and darker when wet. Green is grass, pale specks are seeds, blue is water (deeper is more opaque), shallow running water shows as thin bright blue, a green tint in water is algae (barely visible pale specks drifting in the water are its spores), and white patches are clouds, which turn grey while raining. Wiggling coloured dashes are fish and the larger shark shapes are sharks (colour is genetic, so families share colours). Small pastel rounded squares with round black heads on land are sheep, and the bigger, longer dark shapes with curly tails are cats. Big tawny birds are rocs: wings spread with a shadow beneath while flying, folded when landed. Dead animals turn grey and fade away as they rot. Gardeners are little pixel people in coloured cloaks, swinging their arms as they walk; their wooden boat is drawn under them while they row, over their head while they carry it, and on the shore where they leave it. A faint dashed line shows where they're heading, and a thin line flies out when they throw a spear.

        The gardener

        A person who roams the world trying to keep every species alive. They can see the whole map and every population. Whenever they're free, the game lists a handful of things they could do, and they pick one. They survey the separate land masses and lakes and what lives in each. They know who eats what, and they follow each population's total and trend, judging for themselves which are rare or falling. Their options are about particular places: cull the hunters where a struggling species lives, move animals from where there's no food to where there is, reunite lone animals, start new groups in good empty places, thin out grazers stripping their plants, or sow bare ground. The card shows how they see each population and place. Rocs are sacred: they never hunt, cull or carry them. They choose by built-in rules that score each option, or, if you choose it on the Gardener card, by a tiny open language model (SmolLM2-135M-Instruct) running in your browser, which reads the situation and the options as text and answers with a number. If the model can't answer, the rules decide. They walk fast, row a boat on water, and carry the boat over land (slowly) when a route needs it, planning the fastest way. They cast a net over schools of fish (it lands a spear's throw ahead of them and catches every fish under it), then carry the catch to another lake and let it go, or eat it. They eat anything but spores, store lots of fat, and starve if it runs out. Set how many gardeners there are with Gardeners on the Settings tab; several share the work, avoid chasing the same animal, and spread out. Pick which one to follow on the Gardener card.
